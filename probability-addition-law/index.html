F Addition law
Compound events

Two events A and B in a sample space U

A ∩ B: both A and B occur

read: “A intersection B”

A ∪ B: A or B or both occur

read: “A union B”

F Addition law
Counting first

U = {1, 2, …, 99}

A: multiples of 7, B: multiples of 5

n(A) = 14, n(B) = 19
n(A ∩ B) = 2 (35 and 70)
n(A ∪ B) = 31, not 14 + 19 = 33

35 and 70 are counted twice.

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)

3/16
F Addition law
The addition law of probability

Dividing by n(U) gives the addition law:

Addition law
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

P(either A or B or both) = P(A) + P(B) − P(both A and B)

P(A ∩ B) is subtracted because it is contained in P(A) and in P(B).

F Addition law
Example 13

If P(A) = 0.6, P(A ∪ B) = 0.7 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.3, find P(B).

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
0.7 = 0.6 + P(B) − 0.3

P(B) = 0.4

F Addition law
Mutually exclusive events
Definition

A and B are mutually exclusive (disjoint) if they cannot occur at the same time:

A ∩ B = ∅, so P(A ∩ B) = 0

For mutually exclusive events the addition law becomes

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
6/16
F Addition law
Example 14

30 students write a History test: 7 score an A, 11 score a B. One student is selected at random.

A: the student scored an A, B: the student scored a B

a) Are A and B mutually exclusive? b) Find P(A), P(B), P(A ∩ B) and P(A ∪ B).

a) No student can score both an A and a B, so A and B are mutually exclusive.
b) P(A) = 730, P(B) = 1130, P(A ∩ B) = 0
P(A ∪ B) = 730 + 1130 = 1830 = 35

F Exercise 1
Using the addition law

P(A) = 0.35, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.6

a) Find P(A ∩ B). b) Find the probability that neither A nor B occurs. c) Are A and B mutually exclusive?

a) P(A ∩ B) = 0.35 + 0.5 − 0.6 = 0.25
b) P((A ∪ B)′) = 1 − 0.6 = 0.4
c) No, since P(A ∩ B) = 0.25 ≠ 0.

Independent events

A and B are independent ⟺ P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)

11/16
G Independent events
Example

A and B are independent with P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A ∩ B) and P(A ∪ B).

P(A ∩ B) = 0.3 · 0.5 = 0.15
P(A ∪ B) = 0.3 + 0.5 − 0.15 = 0.65
Careful

Mutually exclusive does not mean independent. If P(A) > 0, P(B) > 0 and A, B are mutually exclusive, then P(A ∩ B) = 0 ≠ P(A) · P(B): if A occurs, B cannot occur.

G Exercise 3
Testing for independence

P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Are A and B independent?

P(A ∩ B) = 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.7 = 0.2
P(A) · P(B) = 0.4 · 0.5 = 0.2

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B), so A and B are independent.

Two archers

Anna and Ben shoot at a target independently. Anna hits with probability 0.7, Ben with probability 0.6.

Find the probability that a) both hit, b) at least one hits, c) neither hits.

a) P(A ∩ B) = 0.7 · 0.6 = 0.42
b) P(A ∪ B) = 0.7 + 0.6 − 0.42 = 0.88
c) P(A′ ∩ B′) = 0.3 · 0.4 = 0.12 (check: 1 − 0.88)
